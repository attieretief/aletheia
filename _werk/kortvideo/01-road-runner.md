# Kortvideo 1 — "Daar is geen waarheid nie" is nie waar nie
Bron: Proloog, opstel 3 (*Wie se Waarheid?*), §2 en slot.
Na die kamera. 2½–3 minute. Afrikaans (Engelse weergawe onder).
Stel: by 'n braai, foon op armlengte, natuurlike lig. Een stut: die braaitang.
Titelkaart aan die einde: *aletheia.attieretief.com*

---

**[Koue opening. Fluister. Foon baie naby. Kyk vinnig oor die skouer na die vuur. Tang laag.]**

Psst. Kom nader.

**[Kyk weer om.]**

Ek gaan jou iets vertel wat 'n mens nie by 'n braai sê nie.

Jy ken die ou. Hy sit sy bier neer en sê: "Kyk, ek het niks teen jou geloof nie. As dit vir jou
werk, is dit jou waarheid. Ek het myne. *You do you.*"

Almal knik. Dit klink gaaf. Niemand wil die een wees wat die aand bederf nie.

Hier's die geheim...

**[Pouse.]**

...niemand leef eintlik so nie.

**[Normale stem. Tree terug van die foon af.]**

As elkeen sy eie waarheid het, is daar dan nog iets soos *die* waarheid? Kom ek wys jou.

**[Titelkaart: "Daar is geen waarheid nie" is nie waar nie]**

**[Stadiger. Die meganisme.]**

Kom ons vat die skerpste weergawe van wat hy sê, en kyk wat gebeur as ons dit ernstig opneem.

"Daar is geen waarheid nie."

Is *dit* waar?

As dit waar is, is daar ten minste een waarheid. En dan is dit vals.

As dit nie waar is nie, hoef ons ons nie daaraan te steur nie.

Of probeer die sagter een: "Alle waarheid is relatief." Geld dit vir almal, oral, altyd? Dan is dit
'n absolute waarheid. En die stelling weerlê homself.

**[Glimlag.]**

Die Amerikaanse apologete Norman Geisler en Frank Turek noem dit die Road Runner-taktiek. Onthou jy die
tekenprent? Wile E. Coyote jaag die voël, hardloop reguit oor die rand van 'n krans, en bly net daar
in die lug hang. Hy val eers wanneer hy afkyk en sien daar is niks onder hom nie.

'n Stelling wat nie aan sy eie maatstaf voldoen nie, hang net so in die lug. Dit hou solank
niemand afkyk nie.

**[Leun nader. Die draai.]**

Maar hier moet ek eerlik wees. Dié argument slaan die *slagspreuk* plat. Dit slaan nie die man by die vuur plat
nie.

Want wat hy eintlik bedoel, is nie 'n filosofiese teorie nie. Hy bedoel: waar jy staan, bepaal wat
jy sien. En dit is waar. 'n Boer in die Karoo en 'n bankier in Sandton kyk anders na dieselfde
droogte. Ons kyk almal van êrens af.

Maar onthou: as ons almal van êrens af kyk, is daar iets om na te kyk. Twee mense wat van verskillende kante
na dieselfde berg kyk, sien nie twee berge nie. Hulle sien een berg, van twee kante.

As ek met die Road Runner-taktiek wen en dan dink die gesprek is verby, het ek nie geluister nie.

**[Pouse. Kyk weg van die kamera, asof na die vuur.]**

**[Klein einde.]**

En kyk wat gebeur later dieselfde aand. Die vleis is amper klaar. Dieselfde man prik die hoender
met 'n vurk en frons.

"Is hierdie ding gaar?"

Niemand antwoord: "Dit hang af van jou waarheid."

**[Leun weer naby die foon. Fluister. Kyk oor die skouer.]**

Oor die hoender wil niemand sy eie waarheid hê nie.

**[Titelkaart: aletheia.attieretief.com]**

# Short 1 — "There's no truth" isn't true
Source: Prologue, essay 3 (*Whose Truth?*), §2 and ending.
To camera. 2½–3 minutes. English.
Setting: at a braai, phone at arm's length, natural light. One prop: the braai tongs.
End title card: *aletheia.attieretief.com*

---

**[Cold open. Whisper. Phone very close. Quick glance over the shoulder toward the fire. Tongs held low.]**

Psst. Come closer.

**[Glance again.]**

I'm going to tell you something you're not supposed to say at a braai.

You know the guy. He puts down his beer and says: "Look, I've got nothing against your faith. If it
works for you, it's your truth. I've got mine. You do you."

Everyone nods. It sounds kind. Nobody wants to be the one who spoils the evening.

Here's the secret...

**[Pause.]**

...nobody actually lives like that.

**[Normal voice. Step back from the phone.]**

If everyone has their own truth, is there still such a thing as *the* truth? Let me show you.

**[Title card: "There's no truth" isn't true]**

**[Slower. The mechanism.]**

Let's take the sharpest version of what he's saying, and see what happens when we take it seriously.

"There is no truth."

Is *that* true?

If it is, then there is at least one truth. And then it's false.

If it isn't true, we needn't worry about it.

Or try the softer one: "All truth is relative." Does that hold for everyone, everywhere, always? Then
it's an absolute truth. And the statement refutes itself.

**[Smile.]**

The American apologists Norman Geisler and Frank Turek call this the Road Runner tactic. Remember the cartoon? Wile
E. Coyote chases the bird, runs straight off the edge of a cliff, and just hangs there in mid-air. He
only falls when he looks down and sees there's nothing underneath him.

A statement that fails its own standard hangs in the air just like that. It holds only as long as
nobody looks down.

**[Lean in. The turn.]**

But I have to be honest here. This argument knocks down the *slogan*. It doesn't knock down the man
by the fire.

Because what he really means isn't a philosophical theory. He means: where you stand shapes what you
see. And that's true. A farmer in the Karoo and a banker in Sandton look at the same drought
differently. We all see from somewhere.

Only: if we all see from somewhere, there is something to see. Two people looking at the same
mountain from different sides don't see two mountains. They see one mountain, from two sides.

If I win with the Road Runner tactic and think the conversation is over, I haven't been listening.

**[Pause. Look away from the camera, as if at the fire.]**

**[Small ending.]**

And watch what happens later that same evening. The meat's nearly done. The same man prods the
chicken with a fork and frowns.

"Is this thing cooked?"

Nobody answers, "That depends on your truth."

**[Lean back in close. Whisper. Glance over the shoulder.]**

When it comes to the chicken, nobody wants their own truth.

**[Title card: aletheia.attieretief.com]**
